# Easy-access and notice savings (UK)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

**Easy-access savings** at banks and building societies hold cash you may need on
short notice, often with variable interest. **Notice accounts** pay different rates
but require advance notice (for example 30 or 90 days) before withdrawal. Both are
deposit products when offered by **FCA-authorised** firms — not investments.

## Who it is typically designed for

Households building an **emergency buffer** and managing household bills. Compare
**AER**, bonus rates that expire, and minimum deposits. Universal Credit and other
benefit households should understand how savings balances affect means tests using
official GOV.UK guidance, not forum guesses.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Easy-access:** withdrawals often same-day or next-day within limits; some accounts
  cap penalty-free withdrawals per year.
- **Notice:** plan ahead — emergencies may force breaking notice at a penalty.

## Risks

- **Introductory rates** dropping after 12 months without you noticing.
- **FSCS coverage** limits and firm eligibility — learn from FSCS, not branch posters alone.
- Fraud (fake bank texts, courier scams).

## Literacy angle

Use FCA and MoneyHelper-style official resources to compare **deposit protection**
and fees. Pair with [Cash ISA](cash-isa.md) literacy only after you understand basic
access needs.

## Official source types

- Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) consumer guidance
- Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS)
- Bank of England context on inflation
