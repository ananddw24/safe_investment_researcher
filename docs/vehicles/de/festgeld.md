# Festgeld (Germany)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

**Festgeld** is a **fixed-term bank deposit**: you agree on an amount, duration,
and interest rate for the term. At maturity you typically receive principal plus
contracted interest according to the product sheet. It is a deposit product, not
a stock or fund — but early access rules still matter.

## Who it is typically designed for

Households with surplus cash they will **not** need for known expenses during the
term (for example, a tax bill date you can calendar). It is a poor place for
**emergency money** because breaking the term early may cost interest or be
disallowed except on bank-specific terms.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Low to medium** until maturity; **vorzeitige Kündigung** (early termination)
  may reduce returns or require bank consent.
- **Automatic renewal** at maturity may roll into a new rate — confirm opt-in/out.
- Deposit protection (**Einlagensicherung**) applies to eligible deposits up to
  legal limits — learn the scheme from official BaFin/Bundesbank materials.

## Risks

- Locking funds you later need, forcing expensive overdraft or consumer credit.
- **Reinvestment risk** when the term ends and new rates differ.
- Confusing Festgeld with **structured products** or funds sold at the same counter.

## Literacy angle

Match the term to a **dated goal** only after [Giro/Tagesgeld](giro-tagesgeld.md)
covers emergencies. Compare gross vs net interest and tax reporting obligations
using official guidance, not social-media rate tables.

## Official source types

- BaFin deposit protection and consumer information
- Bank product sheet and Einlagensicherung disclosure
