# Regulated mutual funds / Fonds (Germany)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

**Investmentfonds** (mutual funds) pool money to buy stocks, bonds, or other
assets under a regulated **KVG** (capital management company) structure. Fund
units have a **daily or periodic price (NAV)** that can fall. Banks, insurers,
and online brokers **distribute** funds but the fund itself is governed by
prospectus law — not deposit rules.

## Who it is typically designed for

Households with emergency cash already in deposits and a horizon long enough to
weather market swings. Cost-sensitive savers should compare **TER**, front-end
loads, and platform fees using the **KID/PRIIP** or fund factsheet — literacy,
not performance chasing.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Redemption** on fund rules, usually several business days to cash in your account.
- **Not deposit-insured** — different from [Giro/Tagesgeld](giro-tagesgeld.md).
- Some share classes carry **lock-ups** or minimum holding periods — read before signing.

## Risks

- **Capital loss**, especially equity-heavy funds.
- **Fee drag** reducing long-term outcomes.
- **Suitability:** marketing past returns without stress scenarios.
- Tax reporting obligations on distributions and sales — use official tax guidance.

## Literacy angle

Ask: What is the **worst plausible year** in the prospectus stress test? Can I
access this money when my emergency fund runs out? BaFin’s investor education
pages explain key fund vocabulary before you sign.

## Official source types

- BaFin fund supervision and investor education
- Fund prospectus, KID, and annual report
