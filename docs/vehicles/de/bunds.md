# Bunds / federal securities (Germany)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

**Bundesanleihen (Bunds)** are federal government bonds. Households may encounter
government debt literacy through **retail programs** (for example historically
**Bundesschatzbriefe / Bundesschatzanweisungen** and related offerings) sold via
[Finanzagentur](https://www.deutsche-finanzagentur.de/en/) and partner banks.
Terms, minimums, and availability change — always read the current issuance notice.

## Who it is typically designed for

Savers learning how **sovereign debt** works after building liquid emergency cash.
Government securities are not a substitute for payment accounts and still carry
**interest-rate and inflation** literacy considerations.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Primary issuance** may have subscription windows; **secondary market** prices
  move if you sell before maturity via exchanges or bank desks.
- Holding to maturity returns principal per bond terms **if the issuer pays** —
  credit risk for German federal securities is a policy topic, not zero literacy work.

## Risks

- **Fake bond websites** and social-media “exclusive Bund coupons.”
- Buying long maturities without understanding **price volatility** if you must sell early.
- Confusing **Bund funds/ETFs** with direct retail savings products — different mechanics and fees.

## Literacy angle

Use Finanzagentur and Bundesbank educational materials for issuance calendars and
definitions. Trace any purchase to an authorized bank list on official pages.

## Official source types

- Deutsche Finanzagentur (federal debt management)
- Deutsche Bundesbank monetary and education resources
