# CMA-licensed money market funds

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

Money market funds sold to retail investors in Kenya should be **licensed** under
the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) framework. Unit values can move; these are not
bank deposits. Licensing checks are the core literacy habit.

## Who it is typically designed for

People with emergency cash who understand NAV risk and who will verify a fund’s
license on official CMA materials. (If the CMA site is temporarily unreachable,
defer until you can confirm licensing through official channels — do not rely on
WhatsApp lists.)

## Liquidity and commitments

- Redemption rules and cut-off times are fund-specific.
- Fees and yield presentations can confuse — read the fact sheet.

## Risks

- Capital is not guaranteed; “money market” is not “no risk.”
- Unlicensed copycat funds.
- Inflation may exceed advertised yields.

## Literacy angle

Teach the licensing check before any contribution, and the difference between
funds and deposits.

## Official source types

- Capital Markets Authority of Kenya (verify on official CMA channels)
- CBK financial education for adjacent consumer habits
