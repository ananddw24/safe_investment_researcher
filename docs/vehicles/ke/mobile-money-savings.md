# Mobile money savings and lock products

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

Mobile money wallets (widely associated with services such as M-PESA) let many
Kenyan households store and move small balances. Some offer **lock** or savings-like
features. These are payment-system products under Central Bank of Kenya oversight
frameworks — not the same as a bank deposit with deposit insurance vocabulary.

## Who it is typically designed for

People who need everyday payments and a first formal balance, including rural users
and women who may rely on phone-based access. Agent networks expand reach; they
also create fraud surfaces.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Liquidity:** typically high for wallet balances; lock products reduce access
  for a stated period (read the provider’s published terms).
- Fees for send, withdraw, and lock features can be large relative to small
  balances — learn the tariff table.

## Risks

- SIM-swap and PIN sharing fraud.
- Confusing wallet float with an insured bank deposit.
- Unregulated “investment” apps riding on mobile-money reputation.

## Literacy angle

Teach PIN safety, fee reading, and asking whether a feature is a payment float or
a regulated deposit. Use CBK and Communications Authority materials for system
context.

- Central Bank of Kenya (payments / financial education)
- Communications Authority of Kenya (sector context)
