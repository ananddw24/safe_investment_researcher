# Ordinary bank and Japan Post Bank deposits

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

**Futsū yokin** (ordinary deposits) at commercial banks and **Japan Post Bank**
are the default place for wages, pensions, and everyday cash. They support
transfers, cash cards, and automatic bill payment. This is household **plumbing**,
not a growth investment.

Japan Post Bank and city banks differ in branch hours and product menus, but deposit
regulation and disclosure expectations apply to licensed institutions.

## Who it is typically designed for

Nearly all households in the formal system, including seniors receiving pension
deposits. Part-time and irregular workers should watch **maintenance fees** and
minimum-balance rules that can erode small accounts.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **High** for ordinary deposits within daily transfer and ATM limits.
- **Deposit insurance** literacy: learn current coverage limits and categories from
  official JFSA and institution disclosures — do not assume unlimited protection.

## Risks

- **Counter confusion:** investment trusts or insurance sold beside deposits.
- **Furikomi fraud** and phone scams impersonating banks or family members.
- Card skimming and lost passbook/IC card misuse.

## Literacy angle

Keep emergency cash here or in equally accessible accounts before [time deposits](time-deposits.md),
[NISA](nisa.md), or [iDeCo](ideco.md). Ask for written confirmation that a product is
a **yokin** (deposit), not a fund.
