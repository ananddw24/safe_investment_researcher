# Time deposits / teiki (Japan)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

**Teiki yokin** (time deposits) lock a set amount for a published term and interest
rate at a bank or Japan Post Bank. They are still **deposits**, but maturity and
early-cancellation rules differ from ordinary accounts.

## Who it is typically designed for

Savers with calendar-known expenses who will not need the principal during the
term. Poor fit for **emergency buffers** because pre-maturity access may forfeit
interest or require bank-specific penalties.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Lower** until maturity; early termination terms are on the account contract.
- Automatic renewal clauses can roll funds into a new term at a **new rate** — confirm
  instructions at opening.

## Risks

- Locking cash needed for medical or job-loss shocks.
- **Inflation literacy:** nominal rate vs purchasing power over the term.
- Confusing promotional “campaign rates” with long-term access needs.

## Literacy angle

Fund [ordinary deposits](bank-postal-deposits.md) first, then match teiki length to
dated goals (tax, tuition) — not to social pressure for “higher rate today.”

## Official source types

- JFSA banking consumer materials
- Institution time-deposit pamphlet
