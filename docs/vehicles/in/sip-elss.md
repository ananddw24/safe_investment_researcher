# SIP and ELSS mutual funds (literacy)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

Mutual funds pool money into portfolios managed by Asset Management Companies
under **SEBI** regulation. A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a *method* of
investing fixed amounts at intervals. Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) are a
category of equity-oriented funds that have historically carried a lock-in (commonly
discussed as about three years — **confirm current SEBI/fund documents**).

## Who it is typically designed for

People who already have emergency savings and understand that **unit values can
fall**. Not a first step for someone who may need the money within months.

## Liquidity and commitments

- Open-end equity funds: typically redeemable on market days, but prices move daily.
- ELSS: lock-in applies to each installment as per scheme documents.
- SIPs can be stopped; that does not remove market risk on units already held.

## Risks

- Capital loss; past returns do not predict future results.
- Expense ratios and commissions reduce net outcomes — learn to read the total
  expense ratio and avoid tip-driven churning.
- Fake “guaranteed mutual fund return” pitches are a scam pattern.

## Literacy angle

Use SIP as a story about **habit and volatility**, not about getting rich. Point
learners to SEBI investor education and AMFI — not WhatsApp tips.

## Official source types

- SEBI Investor Education / Investor Portal
- AMFI
- Individual scheme information documents (SID/KIM)

See [SEBI Investor Portal](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/), [SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/),
[AMFI](https://www.amfiindia.com/), [BSE Investors](https://bseindia.com/investors/).
