# Public Provident Fund (PPF)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

The Public Provident Fund is a **long-horizon small-savings scheme** of the
Government of India, offered through banks and post offices. Contribution limits,
interest notification, and withdrawal/loan rules are set by government notification
and can change — always read the current official terms.

## Who it is typically designed for

Households who can set aside money they will not need for many years. Often
discussed in literacy materials around retirement or long family goals. Tax
treatment has historically been framed as EEE-style in popular guides — **tax rules
change**; verify with current Income Tax / DFS materials, not blogs.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Liquidity:** low — designed around a multi-year lock-in with limited partial
  withdrawal/loan windows as published.
- Annual contribution ceilings apply; unused room typically does not carry forward
  the way some other accounts do — confirm current rules before planning.

## Risks

- Locking emergency money inside PPF creates hardship risk.
- Interest is administratively set; it is not “market alpha.”
- Documentation and nomination mistakes are costly over long horizons.

## Literacy angle

PPF is useful for teaching **goal-based saving**, compounding over long periods,
and the difference between bank deposits and government small-savings schemes.

## Official source types

- Department of Financial Services / Ministry of Finance scheme materials
- India Post and agency bank PPF pages
- Income Tax Department for current tax treatment

Portals to start with: [Financial Services](https://financialservices.gov.in/),
[India Post](https://www.indiapost.gov.in/), [myScheme](https://www.myscheme.gov.in/),
[Income Tax](https://www.incometax.gov.in/).
