# SHG and microfinance literacy

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and joint-liability or microfinance arrangements are common
**group savings and credit** structures, especially among women. Some entities are
regulated as deposit-taking or as NBFC-MFIs; others are informal. The literacy goal
is to separate **saving**, **borrowing**, and **group pressure**.

## Who it is typically designed for

Women and low-income households seeking community savings discipline or small
credit. Access can be empowering — and also a channel for over-indebtedness.

## Liquidity and commitments

- Group savings schedules create social commitment.
- Loans create repayment schedules; multiple loans from overlapping groups is a
  known risk pattern discussed in conduct norms.

## Risks

- Over-indebtedness and coercive collection practices (document as risk literacy).
- Confusing a loan with “savings growth.”
- Unregulated chit or tip clubs branded as SHGs.

## Literacy angle

Ask: Is this entity regulated? Are rates and fees written? Can I leave the group
without losing deposits unfairly? RBI microfinance conduct norms and NCFE materials
are better teachers than peer pressure.

## Official source types

- RBI (microfinance / NBFC conduct and consumer education)
- NABARD (SHG linkage literature — confirm current portal pages when available)
- NCFE financial education

See [RBI Financial Education](https://www.rbi.org.in/FinancialEducation/),
[NCFE](https://ncfe.org.in/), [RBI notifications index](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/English/Scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=2313).
