# Recurring Deposit (RD)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

A Recurring Deposit lets you put a **fixed amount each month** into a bank (or
some postal) product for a chosen tenure. Interest terms are set by the issuer
and can change for new bookings over time.

## Who it is typically designed for

Households building a habit around a known future expense (school fees, festival,
appliance) when they can commit to monthly deposits. Irregular-income workers
should be careful: missed installments can bring penalties.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **Liquidity:** medium — money is meant to stay for the term; premature closure
  rules and penalties apply.
- Do **not** lock money you may need for medical or job shocks — that belongs in
  emergency savings first.

## Risks

- Penalty or interest reduction if installments are missed or the RD is broken early.
- Nominal interest may not keep up with inflation (see the inflation guide).
- Auto-debit from a low balance can trigger fees elsewhere.

## Literacy angle

RD teaches **commitment devices**: match tenure to a dated goal, size the
installment so essentials are never skipped, and keep a separate emergency buffer.

## Official source types

- Bank product disclosures and schedule of charges
- RBI consumer education on banking products
- India Post (where a postal RD variant is offered)

Start from [RBI Financial Education](https://www.rbi.org.in/FinancialEducation/) and your
bank or [India Post](https://www.indiapost.gov.in/) published product pages.
