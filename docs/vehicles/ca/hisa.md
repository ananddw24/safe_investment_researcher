# High-interest savings accounts (Canada)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.


## What it is

A **high-interest savings account (HISA)** at a bank or credit union pays interest on
accessible cash, often with no or low fees when marketed to retail savers. Eligible
deposits at **CDIC member institutions** may qualify for **deposit insurance up to
$100,000 per category** — learn categories from CDIC, not branch slogans.

## Who it is typically designed for

Households building emergency funds and storing short-term goals. Compare **promotional
rates** that drop after months versus **ongoing rates**, and whether transfers are
instant between your chequing and HISA at the same institution.

## Liquidity and commitments

- **High** for standard HISAs; some promotional accounts cap balance tiers.
- **GICs** (term deposits) are separate — lower liquidity — see government retail debt page
  for bond/GIC literacy distinction.

## Risks

- **CDIC limit** misunderstandings when spreading money across subsidiaries incorrectly.
- Fraud (Interac e-Transfer scams, SIM swap).
- Keeping chequing and savings in one pooled mental “balance” and overspending.

## Literacy angle

FCAC’s account comparison tools and CDIC’s **deposit insurance calculator** help verify
coverage. Fund emergencies here before [RRSP](rrsp.md) or invested [TFSA](tfsa.md).

## Official source types

- Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC)
