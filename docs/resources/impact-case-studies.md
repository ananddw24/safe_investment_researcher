# Impact case studies

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.

A regular amount is hard to feel while it is still a rate. These three
worked examples take rhythms a person might actually keep, lay them onto
real calendar months beginning 1 January 2027, and watch what they become —
when the pile crosses each round number, and how each year adds to the last.
Every figure is arithmetic on the stated rhythm, computed exactly the way
the [Impact calendar calculator](tools.md#impact-calendar-calculator) on the
Explore & tools page computes it. No products are named and nothing is
recommended; the subject is the size and shape of the budget itself.

## Case one — The Ten-Dollar Day

Ten dollars a day is an easy amount to picture — a coffee and something
with it, a short ride across town — and an easy amount to dismiss. Laid on
a calendar it is $3,650 a year. The calendar leaves its fingerprints on the
rhythm: February, with only twenty-eight days, contributes $280, while the
seven long months contribute $310 each. The pile passes $500 before
February is out, reaches $1,000 in April, doubles that by July, crosses
$3,000 in October, and closes the year at $3,650.

| Per day | Per week | Per month (average) | Per year |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $10 | $70 | ≈ $304 | $3,650 |

## Case two — The Weekly Rhythm

Twenty-five dollars a week is a rhythm tied to the week's own turning —
set aside when the week begins, the way rent and groceries are weekly
facts of life. It feels smaller than the daily case; across five years it
is not. Counted by whole calendar months, the weekly amount spreads evenly
at $108.33 a month — $1,300 a year, every year the same — and 260 weeks add
up to $6,500. The first $1,000 lands in October 2027, ten months in; after
that the pile crosses another thousand roughly every nine months, regular
as the rhythm itself.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $25 | ≈ $3.57 | $108.33 | $1,300 |

## Case three — The Long View

A hundred dollars a month is the rhythm of a bill — the size of a utility
payment, arriving with the month's other obligations. This case gives it a
head start of $1,000 and twenty years to run, and asks the question the
first two cases deliberately did not: what if the pile, as it grows, also
grows? The growth here is an **assumption for illustration — 5% a year,
drawn as the smooth line such assumptions always draw. It is not a
prediction or a promised return**; real savings and investments do not grow
in a smooth line, and some years arrive negative.

With that assumption, the total reaches $43,816.01. Of that, $25,000 was
there because someone put it there — the $1,000 start plus $24,000 in
monthly contributions — and $18,816.01 is the illustration's growth. The
shape teaches something true about compounding: the second decade adds
$26,640.77 while the first added $16,175.24, because by then the pile
itself is doing part of the work. In 2027 the illustration adds $79.05; by
2046 it adds $2,100.72 in a single year — more than that year's $1,200 of
contributions.

## Try your own rhythm

Every figure on this page came from three choices: an amount, a rhythm,
and a stretch of calendar. The
[Impact calendar calculator](tools.md#impact-calendar-calculator) makes the
same arithmetic with your choices instead — preset rhythms or your own
amount, any start date, horizons from one to forty years, and an optional
growth assumption labelled there, as here, as an illustration. It runs
entirely in your browser; nothing is stored or sent anywhere.

## How the arithmetic works

Contributions are counted by whole calendar months from the start month —
a daily amount is multiplied by the number of days in each month, and a
weekly amount by 52 ÷ 12 each month. Growth, when set above zero, is
applied each month at one twelfth of the annual rate to the balance
carried into that month, before the month's contributions are added.
Milestones are the first month whose end-of-month total reaches each mark.
Amounts are shown with a $ sign; the arithmetic is the same in any currency.
