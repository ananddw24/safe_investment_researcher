# Emergency savings

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.

## What this is

This guide explains why households benefit from a **small, accessible cash
buffer** before locking money into long schemes.

## What this is not

It is not a target amount for your family. It is not advice to open a specific
bank product.

New words: [emergency fund](../glossary.md#emergency-fund) ·
[liquidity](../glossary.md#liquidity).

## Core ideas

1. **Shocks are normal.** Illness, job gaps, crop failure, or urgent travel can
   arrive before a maturity date.
2. **Access matters.** Money you cannot reach without large penalties is not an
   [emergency fund](../glossary.md#emergency-fund).
3. **Separate the jar.** Keep emergency money apart from festival saving or
   long-term schemes so you do not “borrow” from the wrong bucket.
4. **Fees hurt small balances.** Read minimum-balance and SMS charges. A “free”
   account with surprise fees is not free.

## Risks and red flags

- Tips that say “skip emergency savings and put everything in a high-return
  scheme.”
- Borrowing from a microfinance loan to fund a long lock-in product.
- Sharing OTPs or PINs to “release” locked savings.
