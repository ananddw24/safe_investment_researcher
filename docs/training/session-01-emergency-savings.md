# Session 1 — Emergency savings (75 minutes)

!!! warning "Educational only"
    This page is for financial literacy. It is **not** personalized advice,
    an offer to sell, or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold any product.
    Rules differ by country; verify with official sources in your jurisdiction.

## Who this is for

Community trainers facilitating workshops for **low-income households and
women**. Participants may be new to formal banking.

## Learning objectives

By the end, participants can:

1. Explain what an [emergency fund](../glossary.md#emergency-fund) is in plain
   words.
2. Name why [liquidity](../glossary.md#liquidity) matters before long lock-ins.
3. List at least two fee or fraud risks from the guide.
4. Find one official consumer-education link for their country (not a tipster).

## Timed steps (75 minutes)

| Minutes | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Welcome. Read the [Educational disclaimer](../disclaimer.md). State: this is literacy, not advice. |
| 5–15 | Icebreaker: “What sudden cost surprised your household in the last year?” No amounts required. |
| 15–35 | Walk through [Emergency savings](../guides/emergency-savings.md): shocks, access, separate jar, fees. |
| 35–45 | Glossary pause: [emergency fund](../glossary.md#emergency-fund) and [liquidity](../glossary.md#liquidity). |
| 45–60 | Small groups: invent a fictional shock; decide whether locked savings would help or hurt. Report one sentence each. |
| 60–70 | Open [Where to check official figures](../about/where-to-check.md) or the [resource catalog](../resources/catalog.md). Bookmark one **official** site for the group’s country. |
| 70–75 | Close: three takeaways on the board. Remind participants to verify on official sites. |

## Discussion prompts

- Why might a “higher rate” product still be a poor place for emergency money?
- What fees have people seen on small balances (share patterns, not bank names if preferred)?
- How can caregivers keep emergency money reachable without sharing OTPs?

## Do not

!!! danger "Facilitator do-not box"
    - Do not pick a bank, wallet, or scheme for participants
    - Do not set a “correct” savings amount for anyone’s household
    - Do not collect ID numbers or open accounts during the session
    - Do not promise that any product is safe or high-return
